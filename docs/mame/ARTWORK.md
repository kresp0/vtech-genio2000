# Photo artwork with clickable keys (`artwork/genio2000/`)

A MAME layout that shows the owner's photographs of the unit (lid with the LCD, keyboard and activity panel) with the emulated LCD in the real LCD window. **Evidence class: EMU/tooling, photos = HW.**
It is not part of the PR candidate (the photos are the owner's, MAME's artwork is distributed separately) and the generated files are not committed to the MAME tree.

## Use
```bash
tools/run_genio.sh          # adds -artpath artwork ; the view "Photo (clickable)" is the first one
```
The plain screen view is available from the MAME UI (Scroll Lock toggles UI mode, then Tab > Video Options). The mouse pointer is shown over the picture; a click holds the key while the button is down (highlight in yellow).

## What works with the mouse
| Control | Port / field | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| 56 main keys | `IN0..IN7`, bit = matrix row (generated from `data/teclado_es_source.csv`, same source as the input ports) | PR 2 |
| 39 activity buttons (BASIC, OFF, ...) | `IN8..INF` bits 0-4 | PR 2 |
| **ON** | `POWER` bit 0. OFF (port 0x12 bit 0, HW) halts the CPU and blanks the LCD; ON works **at any time**, on or off, and is reset + RAM clear + power-on (HW, owner): emulated **inside the running MAME** (no MAME reset, so the window and its size are kept, also full screen): the work RAM (0xC000-0xDFFF) is cleared, the output latch, keyboard latch and LCD controller are reset and the CPU RESET line is pulsed, which gives the full start-up sequence | dev only |
| **CONTRASTE** slide switch | `CONTRAST` toggle (type `IPT_OTHER`; a click flips it; the handle is redrawn at the right-hand position) | PR 8 |
| Caps-lock LED | output `led_caps` follows bit 5 of port 0x12 (HW: the LED follows caps-lock) | PR 6 |
| LCD glass margin colour | output `lcd_dark` (contrast position); without it the margin keeps the lit-LCD colour | `genio2000-dev` only |
With a build that lacks a PR the corresponding control is inert (its port or output does not exist; the layout still loads). The photo layout itself is not part of the series.

## Photo preparation (changes of 2026-11-09)
* **Lid:** the photo was slightly crooked and keystoned; it is now warped (OpenCV perspective transform from the corners of the printed blue panel) so the panel is a rectangle, and the crop keeps the whole grey frame, the lower bezel and the hinge so that it joins the keyboard photo.
* **Keyboard:** the top-down photo is also keystoned (the body is 4 % wider at the top than at the bottom); it is warped from the two body edges and cropped to show the complete border of the body, from below the hinge to the whole lower bezel. The clickable rectangles are transformed with the same homography.
* **Printed title bar / second pass:** the four corners of the printed panel found automatically were only approximate: the printed "window title bar" at the top of the lid still sloped 16 px over its width (top edge 101 -> 117) and was 8 px thicker on the right. The four sides are now measured on the first-pass image (title bar top edge, blue bottom edge, left and right blue edges), fitted with lines and their intersections used for a second homography (`refine_panel_homography()`); the title bar is level and of constant thickness (133 +- 1 px, 56 px thick) and the hinge, previously 11 px off the lid axis, ends up within 2 px of it, so the later left/right stretch is only +-0.3 %.
* **Upper plastic frame:** the raised grey frame above the printed panel is photographed at another depth, so its top edge and the dark lip line under it were still about 33 px lower on the right (outer edge 24 px at the left vs 57 px at the right; lip 73 vs 100). Both edges are measured per column and the strip above the panel is remapped so that they are level at the left-hand values; the panel itself does not move (`level_top()` in `tools/gen_artwork.py`).
* **Keyboard photo, second pass and fit to the lid:** the printed header band (JUEGOS DE PALABRAS, MATEMATICAS ...) still sloped 21 px over the width after the first correction. As for the lid, the header's top edge and the blue panel's sides and bottom are fitted with lines and a second homography makes the band level (top edge 94 +- 1 px across the whole width). Then the left/right outer edges of the body are measured per row and the base is stretched with a row-dependent horizontal scale so that its silhouette matches the lid crop at the joint (before: the base was ~10 px narrower at the right; the stretch is under 1 %). The key rectangles go through the same transformations (`refine_base_homography()` and the edge fit in `tools/gen_artwork.py`).
* **Tight crop, same body width at the joint:** each photograph is cropped 2 px beyond its outermost plastic edge, so as little as possible that is not the computer is visible, and no plastic piece is left out: left, right and bottom of the keyboard, and top, left and right of the lid including the pink hinge switches (they stick out of the frame by ~10 px, found on the rectified lid). The lid is scaled so that its frame edges are exactly as far apart as the body edges of the keyboard photo (1841 px; the scale comes from the measured edges); because the switches make the lid view wider, the keyboard photo is placed under it at the x where its body edges sit under the lid's frame edges (the few px at each side are empty). The bottom edge of the keyboard is found where the desk wood starts. View: 1867 x 2580 px.
* **Lid/keyboard alignment (check of 2026-11-09):** the outer edges of both photos coincide within 3 px at the joint (left 4/4, right 1845/1848); the widths of lid and base at the hinge line measured on the whole-unit photo `A_front_overall_2` are within 2 %, so equal widths is a good assumption; the keys + activity panel are centred within 1.5 px of the layout axis. The lid photo, however, was not taken on the axis: its hinge (the middle of the unit) was 11 px right of the middle of the frame. The left and right halves of the lid are stretched by -1.4 % / +1.5 % so that the hinge centre is on the axis (frame edges unchanged); all lid coordinates (LCD, LED, slider) use the same mapping. The LCD module itself stays ~9 px right of the axis, as printed.
* **LCD module:** the LCD bezel stands out of the panel, so after the panel correction it was still slightly rotated/keystoned (its right side was about 7 px lower than the left). The glass is detected on the rectified photo and the area around it (bezel included) is locally stretched with a second homography, blended into the surrounding photo, so the glass, its blue frame and the lavender frame have level edges.
* Both photos are scaled to the same width (lid and body assumed equally wide). Source photos stay untouched in `logs/hardware/`.

## LCD drawing (PR 4)
The LCD is drawn as photographed (no longer an option): every character cell is 6 x 10 dots: glyph rows 1-7, **row 8 never lit**, row 9 = the eighth glyph row (cursor underline, ten symbol codes) drawn like the others, **row 10 = blank line gap** to the next text line; column 6 is the character gap. The screen is 120 x 40 dots (gl4000 keeps 120 x 36). Unlit dots are drawn in a third, faint colour (10 % of the way to a lit dot) so that a powered LCD shows its dot matrix; with CONTRASTE right they are 22 % and the whole LCD is 14 % darker. A thin grid (`lcdgrid.png`) over the screen reproduces the dot pitch. The LCD glass of the photo (the area inside the blue frame, found automatically, including its light bevel line) is covered by a flat rectangle in the colour of the unlit LCD and the dot matrix is inset by a margin of 3 dots on all four sides, so the margins match the powered-off LCD; the rectangle follows the contrast switch through the output `lcd_dark` (dev branch). When powered off all dots disappear. Values are by eye / from one photo pair (not calibrated). 

## Notes on the emulated controls
* ON: **HW (owner, 2026-11-09): after OFF and the farewell message, pressing ON - even briefly - always shows the VTECH title screen**, i.e. a full cold start, so the work RAM is not retained. The emulation matches: a MAME *hard* reset closed and reopened the window (bad in full screen), and a plain *warm* reset with the RAM kept re-displays the farewell instead of the start-up (EMU observation), which is consistent with the real unit losing its RAM; the final implementation clears the RAM and resets the CPU, LCD controller and latches by hand, without a MAME reset. Whether ON is a CPU reset line or the power-up of the whole board is not known.
* CONTRASTE: right = darker LCD with more visible unlit dots (HW photo pair; the owner considers it good as it is). The effect is a 0.86 gain on both LCD colours plus the unlit-dot visibility above, taken from one non-calibrated pair of photographs (mean luminance 80.2 vs 69.2); it is not a model of the LCD bias.
* A mouse holds one key at a time: SHIFT/ALT combinations need the PC keyboard (hold the mapped PC key and click, or use the PC keys).
* **Oval hit areas of the activity buttons:** a layout item can only be a rectangle, so each pill-shaped button is covered by **7 horizontal strips**, each as wide as the pill at that height, all bound to the same input (a click on any strip presses the button; the yellow press highlight therefore also follows the oval). Tested with real mouse clicks: 3, 5 and 7 strips press the button, 9 strips (about 1.2 screen pixels each at the default window size) did **not** reliably register, so 7 is the largest tested number. The hit area is a stepped approximation of the oval, not an exact one (`NSTRIP` in `tools/gen_artwork.py`).
* **Activity panel:** the 40 button rectangles are no longer placed by hand: the pills are detected on the rectified photo (lavender fill / teal ON-OFF pills, `find_panel_buttons()`), because the printed panel is slightly skewed and the hand-made grid drifted from some buttons (RESTA ...). **Main keys:** the hand-placed rectangles are snapped to the dark gaps around each keycap (`refine_key()`, moves of up to 9 px, mean 6 px). `--debug` writes overlay images to `work/` to check both.

## Files
`default.lay`, `lid.png`, `base.png`, `led_on.png`, `slider_right.png`, `lcdgrid.png` are generated by `python3 tools/gen_artwork.py` from `logs/hardware/photos_2026-11-09/`. The right-hand slider position is **edited from the photo of the left position**: an oval that reaches into the **middle of the cream ring** around the slot (so it includes the bevel and the shadow of the opening, and the cut falls in the smooth cream) is selected and flipped horizontally; pixels whose mirror falls outside the oval are inpainted, the cut is feathered, and the outer half of the ring and the blue around it are not touched. The second photograph of the slider is no longer used. The caps LED of the lid photo is painted unlit.
End-to-end check on a real X display with real mouse clicks: `python3 tools/artwork_click_test.py <outdir>` (needs `xdotool`, ImageMagick; it moves the pointer for ~2 min). Result of 2026-11-09: OFF blanks the LCD, ON restarts to the welcome screen, BASIC opens the READY prompt, CONTRASTE moves the slider and darkens the LCD (twice, back to normal): `logs/artwork/*.png`, `click_test_montage.png`.
Automatic tests: `test_artwork_clickable_items_exist_as_input_fields` (all clickable items map to existing input fields); the behaviours themselves are tested in the suite (`test_power_off_blanks_lcd_and_on_restarts_with_full_boot`, `test_on_button_resets_and_clears_ram_at_any_time`, `test_contrast_switch_darkens_the_lcd_by_the_photographed_ratio`, `test_lcd_photographed_drawing_cell_layout`).
