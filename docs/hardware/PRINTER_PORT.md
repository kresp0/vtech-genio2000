# Printer port as a serial link

The DB25 printer port is the only way found so far to move data between the Genio and a PC without opening the unit. A USB-serial adapter wired to two of its pins gives a link in both directions, driven by software from a cartridge. Evidence classes as in `HARDWARE.md` (HW / EMU / FW / HYP / UNK). Unless stated otherwise the HW facts come from unit 1 and the owner's USB-serial adapter on 2026-11-13, with `examples/pc-link` (the dumper used a CP2102; the adapter of these runs was not re-identified).

## Pins and ports
| DB25 pin | Signal | Port | Facts | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | DATA0 (output) | `OUT (10h)` bit 0 | **Inverted**: port bit 0 = 1 drives the pin low, 0 drives it high. A software UART on it is received by a PC at 2400 ... 128000 baud. No STROBE pulse is needed: `pc-link` never touches port 0x12 and the PC receives every byte | HW (dumper 9600 baud; `pc-link` all rates) |
| 3-9 | DATA1-7 | `OUT (10h)` bits 1-7 | Not used by the link; they follow whatever is written | FW |
| 11 | BUSY (input) | `IN (11h)` bit 5 | **Inverted**: reads 1 while the pin is low, 0 while it is high, as HotKey's VGLDK notes say for the GL4000. Unconnected it reads 0 (port 0x11 = `5F` at rest). Driven directly by the adapter's TXD (no resistors) it follows the line up to 128000 baud | HW |
| 1 | STROBE | `OUT (12h)` bit 2 | Not needed for the link | HW (not used) |
| 18-25 | ground | | common with the adapter | HW |

* **Port 0x10 is also the keyboard column select**: bit n = 1 selects column n (`KEYBOARD.md`; the firmware, VGLDK and MAME do it that way, and the keyboard measurements on the unit used it). Selecting column 0 therefore drives DATA0 low, which a PC listening on DATA0 receives as a start bit and garbage. Scanning only columns 1-7 keeps the line idle: `pc-link` scans the keyboard between packets that way and the PC saw 0 stray bytes in every run (HW). The column 0 keys (AYUDA, PAUSA and five activity keys) cannot be read without disturbing the line.
* **Port 0x11 bits 0-4** are matrix 2 (the activity panel) and depend on the selected columns; bit 5 does not. With port 0x10 = 0 no column is selected and bits 0-4 read 1 (no key), so reading bit 5 is not disturbed by keys (HW, `pc-link`).
* `OUT (11h),FF` is written by the firmware at boot and by the tested transmitter after every bit (the GL4000 documentation calls it a latch / write enable). The `pc-link` transmitter at 115200 and 128000 baud never writes it and works; whether it matters at all is UNKNOWN.
* The upper half of the port address does not matter: `IN A,(11h)` puts A on A8-A15 and `INI` puts B there, and both read BUSY (HW).

## What was measured
* **Rates.** Receiving by polling (`IN A,(11h)` with T-state-counted delays): 2400, 4800, 9600, 19200, 38400, 57600 and 76800 baud, every packet of several sweeps answered at the first try (HW). Above that there is not enough time to poll, sample and shift a bit; recording the line with `INI` (one sample every 16 T) and decoding it afterwards worked at 115200 and 128000 baud, 20 of 20 packets, only after correcting the edge asymmetry below (HW). 153600 lost packets even then, and 230400 failed (HW); the method cannot resolve bits shorter than about two samples (EMU, simulation). Transmitting worked at every rate up to 128000 (HW).
* **Throughput** with one packet out and its echo back (stop-and-wait, up to 240 bytes of payload): 106, 204, 372, 632, 974, 1184 and 1336 payload bytes per second each way from 2400 to 76800 baud; about 250 at 115200 and 128000, where the fixed 11.4 ms recording and 0.1 s of decoding dominate (HW).
* **Edge asymmetry of BUSY.** Recording a known pattern at 128000 baud and timing every edge from its byte's start edge: rising edges (pin going high, bit 5 going 0) arrive 11.6 T (1.9 us) earlier than falling ones, relative to where the pattern puts them; spread about 7 T, most of it the 16 T sampling (HW, one recording). In other words the input sees the line go low about 1.9 us late. Whether that is the input circuit, the cable or the adapter is UNKNOWN. It does not matter at 76800 baud and below (a sample in the middle of the bit), it matters with run-length decoding at 115200 and up (a quarter of a bit).
* **Adapter rates.** From the falling edges of the same recordings: 127 714 baud for a requested 128000 (-0.2 %), about 161 000 for a requested 153600 (+4.9 %, with edges spread by two thirds of a bit). Both assume the Genio runs at exactly 6.13179 MHz (HW, indirect).
* **Clock agreement.** The 9600 baud receiver generated with its bit time 1 % ... 7 % longer or shorter works from -5 % to +4 % on the unit, exactly the window it has in MAME with an exact clock: the adapter and the Genio agree to well within 1 % (HW). This is independent support for the 6.13179 MHz of `CLOCK_AND_SOUND.md` §5.9.
* **Break.** Holding the PC's TXD low (a UART break) reads as bit 5 = 1 for as long as it lasts (HW): usable as an out-of-band reset (`pc-link` returns to its default rate after about 2 s).

## Lessons for software on the link
* The Genio cannot listen while it does anything else: no interrupt is tied to BUSY, so a byte that starts while the program updates the LCD or scans the keyboard is lost or received out of frame. A stop-and-wait protocol works if the Genio finishes all its work (screen included) **before** it answers, and if every packet starts with a few `FF` bytes: they have no falling edge inside them, so a receiver that wakes up in the middle of one just waits for the next start bit (EMU, then HW: drawing the screen after the answer lost the next packet on the unit but not in MAME).
* MAME's `genio2000` driver has no printer port. `examples/pc-link/tests/bridge.lua` replaces it with Lua taps: a read tap on port 0x11 returns the PC's UART waveform at the emulated instant of each `IN`, and a write tap on port 0x10 decodes DATA0. The emulated timing is exact enough to test T-state-counted receivers (the transmitted bit measures 104.162 us for 104.167 us at 9600 baud), but the line is ideal, and the bridge delivers the PC's bytes only at frame boundaries, which hides "busy right after answering" bugs. It can delay the rising or falling edges to model the asymmetry above.
* What is not known: the electrical levels and thresholds of BUSY, whether a pull-up or pull-down is fitted, what the other status lines of the DB25 (ACK, PAPER OUT, SELECT) are connected to (port 0x11 bit 6 reads 1 on the unit and 0 in MAME, function unknown), and whether a 3.3 V adapter is read reliably on every unit (it was on unit 1 if its adapter is a 3.3 V one, which was not checked).
